
## Course Overview

This course focuses on extremely hazardous chemicals found in semiconductor fabrication, one of the most chemical-intensive processes in the industry. It covers nine categories of extreme chemical hazards with data sheets for each.

### Learning Objectives

After completing this course, you will be able to:

- Recall important information about nine categories of extremely hazardous chemicals found in semiconductor fabrication
- Use data sheets to answer questions about extremely hazardous chemicals
- Recognize the initial actions to take in the event of extremely hazardous chemical emergencies

## 1. Toxic Hydride Gases

Highly toxic gases that are also flammable and sometimes pyrophoric. Route of entry at very low concentrations is **inhalation**. They have **poor warning properties** because the safe exposure level is less than the odor threshold (you may not smell them before reaching dangerous levels).

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Arsine** (AsH₃)
- **Diborane** (B₂H₆) — pyrophoric at temperatures > 100°F (38°C)
- **Germanium tetrahydride (Germane)** (GeH₄) — pyrophoric in high concentrations
- **Hydrogen selenide** (H₂Se)
- **Hydrogen sulfide** (H₂S)
- **Phosphine** (PH₃) — pyrophoric in high concentrations

### Key Hazard

Poor warning properties: the safe exposure level is **below the odor threshold**. You cannot rely on smell to detect dangerous concentrations.

---

## 2. Corrosive Gases

Primary hazard is **toxic**. They are also corrosive, causing chemical burns on contact with moisture in tissues (eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory tract). Even at **very low concentrations**, corrosive gases can cause harm.

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Ammonia** (NH₃)
- **Boron trichloride** (BCl₃)
- **Hydrogen bromide** (HBr)
- **Hydrogen chloride** (HCl)
- **Tungsten hexafluoride** (WF₆)

---

## 3. Oxidizing Gases

Primary hazard is **toxic**. They do harm at very low concentrations. Some may also be corrosive. They **increase the risk of fire and explosion** when contacting combustible and flammable materials.

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Chlorine** (Cl₂)
- **Chlorine trifluoride** (ClF₃)
- **Fluorine** (F₂)
- **Nitric oxide** (NO)
- **Nitrogen dioxide** (NO₂)
- **Ozone** (O₃)

### Monitoring Example

Ozone detector set to half the IDLH (5 ppm) → set point should be **2.5 ppm**.

---

## 4. Pyrophoric Gases

**Ignite on contact with air**. Primary hazard class is flammable. Releases have led to explosions in some cases. Some deposit byproducts in exhaust lines.

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Silane** (SiH₄) — covered separately below
- **Phosphine** (PH₃)
- **Germanium tetrahydride (Germane)** (GeH₄)
- **Disilane** (Si₂H₆)

### Key Facts

- Pyrophoric gases are flammable **and toxic**
- Phosphine in high concentrations **will ignite**

---

## 5. Silane — Special Focus

Silane is a very common semiconductor gas used as a silicon deposition source. It is **extremely hazardous** because it almost always ignites when contacting air.

### Mechanism

1. Exothermic reaction with air gives off enough heat to ignite the hydrogen
2. **Controlled release:** light brown cloud (silicon dioxide) with a flame (hydrogen burning) in the middle
3. **Slow release danger:** Silane forms a silicon dioxide layer around a bubble of unreacted silane. When this fragile outer layer breaks, the unreacted silane reacts with air, resulting in an **explosion with 9 times the energy of TNT**

### Key Warning

Silane releases are associated with **significant accidents and deaths** in the semiconductor industry.

### Controls

- Monitoring enclosures for cylinders and fittings
- Specialized gas cabinets
- Continuous monitoring systems

---

## 6. Flammable Gases

Not as hazardous as pyrophoric gases, but still a **significant fire risk** during a release.

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Acetylene** (C₂H₂)
- **Carbon monoxide** (CO)
- **Hydrogen** (H₂)
- **Methane** (CH₄)
- **Propane** (C₃H₈)

### Alarm Set Points

Alarm activation / shutdown signal should be set at **25% of the LEL** (low end of the explosive range).

---

## 7. Highly Energetic Materials (Organometallics)

Often known as organometallic materials. NFPA 704 classifies them as **pyrophoric**. At concentrations ≥ 2%, they are also **unstable or water-reactive**. They produce byproducts that may have the same classifications.

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Diethylzinc** (DEZ)
- **Trimethyl aluminum** (TMA)
- **Trimethyl gallium** (TMG)
- **Trimethylindium** (TMI)

### Hazards

A release can cause **explosion** or exposure to **toxic and corrosive vapors**.

---

## 8. Corrosive Liquids

Can cause **painful and penetrating burns**. Can cause violent exothermic reactions when combined with other chemicals. Can corrode containers and piping if not properly maintained. Can expose people to corrosive vapors or chemical burns.

### Chemicals in This Category

- **Hydrochloric acid** (HCl)
- **Hydrofluoric acid** (HF)
- **Sulfuric acid** (H₂SO₄)
- **Nitric acid** (HNO₃)
- **Phosphoric acid** (H₃PO₄)

### Key Hazards

- Violent exothermic reactions when combined with other chemicals
- Corrosion of containers and piping
- Vapors can cause burns

---

## 9. Hazardous Byproducts

Deposition, diffusion, and implant processes are not 100% efficient. Source material deposits on chamber walls, in exhaust lines, and in exhaust system equipment (pumps, valves, abatement equipment).

### Characteristics

- Can be liquid, solid, or semi-solid combination
- **Air or moisture-reactive** byproducts may react violently or create toxic, flammable, and corrosive vapors
- Retain the **same hazards as the source material**
- When **combined**, may become pyrophoric, water-reactive, toxic, flammable, or corrosive — creating **new hazards** beyond the source material

### Precautions

- Follow special precautions when opening systems containing byproducts
- Follow special procedures when transporting chamber and exhaust line parts
- Follow special cleaning procedures to avoid hazardous reactions

## Emergency Response

A release of an extremely hazardous chemical is a **very serious event**.

### If Alarms Activate

1. **Evacuate** to the designated assembly area
2. Your safety is paramount

### If Safe to Do So

- If you see smoke or flames: activate the fire alarm
- You may remotely shut down gas — **never attempt to shut it off at the cylinder**

### If People Have Been Exposed

| Exposure | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Inhaled a chemical | Take them to **fresh air** |
| Chemical burns | Place them in a **safety shower** |

- Make sure **you are safe** before assisting anyone else
- **Avoid becoming an additional casualty**
- Once safe, report relevant information about the event to responders
- Follow directions from responders
