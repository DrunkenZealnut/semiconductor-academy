
## Course Overview

This course covers the hazards of and controls for gases used in semiconductor fabrication facilities. It addresses gas storage, distribution, use, and emergency procedures.

### Learning Objectives

After completing this course, you will be able to:

- Recall the characteristics of chemical hazard categories
- Identify the characteristics of hazardous gases used in semiconductor manufacturing
- Identify the physical hazards of compressed gases and the precautions to be used
- Categorize gases
- Recognize common controls and determine their use
- Recall procedures for emergencies involving hazardous gas releases

---

## 1. Inert Gases

Inert gases are found throughout the fab. They do not pose chemical or physical hazards aside from being a **simple asphyxiant** — harmful only when concentrated enough to reduce oxygen levels.

### Examples

- Compressed nitrogen (N₂)
- Argon (Ar)
- Sulfur hexafluoride (SF₆)

### Oxygen Deprivation Effects

| Oxygen Level | Effects |
|-------------|---------|
| 23.5% ~ 19.5% | Optimal, no physical symptoms |
| 19.5% ~ 10% | Increased heart rate and breathing |
| 10% ~ 6% | Nausea, vomiting, unconsciousness |
| Below 6% | **Deadly** |

## 2. Cryogenic Cylinders (Dewars)

Cryogenic cylinders store cryogenic liquids such as nitrogen, argon, hydrogen, and oxygen. The liquid is stored under pressure to keep it in the liquid phase and evaporates slowly, generating a gas phase for use in clean rooms.

### Thermal Expansion Hazard

- **Liquid nitrogen expansion coefficient: ~700** — every 1 liter of liquid nitrogen creates 700 liters of nitrogen gas
- Dewars in clean rooms typically range from 160 to 230 liters capacity
- A catastrophic release of a full cylinder could generate **up to 161,000 liters of nitrogen gas**, significantly affecting oxygen concentration in the room

### Safety Precautions

- **PPE:** Safety glasses or face shield, insulated gloves, apron when handling cryogenic liquids
- **Seismic safety:** Large dewars must be properly restrained in earthquake-prone areas
- **Monitoring:** Facility should monitor oxygen levels in spaces where cryogenic containers are stored

## 3. Compressed Gas Cylinder Hazards

Pressures inside a full compressed gas cylinder may exceed **2,000 PSI**.

### Physical Hazards

| Hazard | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Gas discharge** | High-speed gas discharge from nozzle or valve stem; debris and dust carried in the stream become flying particle hazards |
| **Pinhole leaks** | High-pressure injection hazard — only 100 PSI needed for a pinhole leak to puncture skin. Inspect using approved leak check methods, **never by hand** |
| **Projectile** | If the valve stem is damaged and shears off, all pressure releases instantaneously, turning the cylinder into a **high-speed projectile** |

### Precautions

- Wear eye protection when connecting gas lines
- Keep cylinder caps on when not in use
- Support cylinders using a cylinder cart
- Wear safety shoes if required by your facility
- **Always secure cylinders** to prevent damage from falling — secure to cart during transport and in place where used

- Inert gases (simple asphyxiants)
- Flammable gases
- Pyrophoric gases
- Oxidizing gases
- Corrosive gases
- Toxic hydride gases
- Highly energetic materials (organometallics)

## 5. Common Gas Controls

Controls used with all compressed gas cylinders regardless of hazard or quantity:

### Infrastructure Controls

- **Emergency shutdown systems** — remote shutdown capability
- **Pressure monitoring devices** — continuous pressure monitoring
- **Pressure relief devices** — automatic pressure release to prevent over-pressurization
- **Compatible materials** — piping and components made of materials compatible with the gas they carry
- **Signs and labeling** — systems and piping areas clearly marked

### NFPA Rating System

The National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) hazard rating system uses numbers 0 to 4:

| Rating | Hazard Level |
|--------|-------------|
| 0 | No hazard |
| 1 | Slight hazard |
| 2 | Moderate hazard |
| 3 | Serious hazard |
| **4** | **High hazard** |

Hazardous gas controls are **more stringent** for gases rated 3 or 4 in any NFPA diamond field.

### Maximum Allowable Quantity (MAQ)

The International Fire Code sets MAQ thresholds for each hazardous gas class. Most semiconductor fabrication facilities store and use quantities **above the MAQ**, making requirements more stringent than typical industrial settings.

## 6. Hazardous Gas Control System — Step-by-Step

A complete hazardous gas control system is built in layers:

| Step | Component | Function |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | **Source gas cylinder** | Contains the hazardous gas |
| 2 | **Restrictive Flow Orifice (RFO)** | Placed in valve outlet; limits gas escape if piping/component fails |
| 3 | **Enclosure** | Containment for gas release + protection from physical hazards (fire, explosion) |
| 4 | **Pneumatic shut-off valve** | At cylinder outlet; enables remote shutdown by personnel or monitoring equipment |
| 5 | **Exhaust connection** | Draws released gas away from personnel |
| 6 | **Flow sensor** | Monitors gas flow rate |
| 7 | **Gas release sensor** | Detects gas leaks |
| 8 | **Monitoring station** | Receives signals from flow sensor, gas sensor, fire alarm, and seismic activation |
| 9 | **Shutdown signal** | From monitoring station to shut-off valve |
| 10 | **Regulator** | Controls gas pressure |
| 11 | **Gas control panel** | System of components for proper and safe gas flow to downstream equipment |
| 12 | **Excess flow device** | Automatically shuts off gas if flow rate exceeds set point — prevents uncontrolled release |

### Inert Purge Gas

Safe work procedures specify using **purge gas to evacuate hazardous gas** before any piping is disconnected. This is a critical step for cylinder changes and maintenance.

## 7. Emergency Procedures for Gas Releases

### Alarm Response

- Fabs and clean rooms have hazardous gas alarms set to activate at levels **lower than allowable exposure levels**
- Personnel have time to evacuate before overexposure
- **Always evacuate the area whenever you hear an alarm**

### Emergency Shut Off (EMO)

- If you see a problem with a hazardous gas or a system running hazardous gas, use the **EMO (Emergency Machine Off) button**
- This shuts down the equipment and stops any release

### Key Rule

When an alarm activates: **evacuate first**. The EMO button should be used when you see a specific problem, not as a first response to every alarm.
