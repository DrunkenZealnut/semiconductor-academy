
## Course Overview

This course covers semiconductor chemical hazards, controls, and emergency actions. It builds on Parts 1A and 1B, which covered physical, health, and environmental hazard classifications.

### Learning Objectives

After completing this course, you will be able to:

- Identify the chemical hazards in semiconductor fabrication facilities
- Recognize common hazard controls in semiconductor fabrication facilities
- Recall the initial actions to take during a hazardous chemical emergency

---

## 1. Fire Triangle and Flammable Chemicals

Fire requires three factors to ignite:

1. **Fuel source** (e.g., a flammable chemical)
2. **Oxidizer** (e.g., oxygen in the air)
3. **Heat / Ignition source** (e.g., a spark)

To keep burning, a fire also requires a fourth element: a **chemical chain reaction**.

In semiconductor fabrication facilities, fires are prevented by **controlling the ignition source**.

---

## 2. Flammable Liquids

It is not the liquid that burns, but the **vapor** from the liquid that mixes with air.

### Examples in Semiconductor Fabrication

- Isopropyl alcohol (IPA)
- Acetone
- Methyl alcohol
- Photoresists
- Photo developers

### Controls

- Prevent flammable vapors from evaporating in open areas
- Prevent open ignition sources
- Use ventilation to remove and dilute vapors
- Keep flammable liquid containers in chemical storage cabinets

### Flash Point

The **minimum temperature** at which a flammable liquid evaporates enough vapor to ignite in the presence of an ignition source.

- **Isopropyl alcohol flash point:** 53°F (11.7°C)
- At normal room temperature, IPA is well above its flash point, meaning even a small spill could cause a significant flash if exposed to heat or an ignition source.

---

## 3. Flammable Gases

Flammable gases released into the air immediately mix with the air. Each gas must be within a specific concentration range to ignite. Some can ignite or explode upon contact with air.

### Examples

- Hydrogen
- Ethane
- Propane
- Acetylene

### Explosive Limits

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **LEL** (Lower Explosive Level) | Too little gas to ignite — below this, not enough fuel |
| **UEL** (Upper Explosive Level) | Too much gas — above this, not enough oxygen |
| **Flammable Range** | Between LEL and UEL — can ignite and burn |

| Gas | LEL | UEL | Flammable Range |
|-----|-----|-----|-----------------|
| Methane | 5% | 15% | Narrow |
| Hydrogen | 4% | 74% | Very wide |
| Acetylene | 2% | 100% | Extremely wide |

Hydrogen and acetylene have wide flammable ranges, so **any release is dangerous**.

### Controls

- Avoid ignition sources near flammable gas use areas
- Install explosion-proof facilities and equipment
- Ensure high ventilation rates
- Flow flammable gases in **closed systems** (from source through piping and equipment, not open to air)

---

## 4. Flammable Solids

Three types used in the semiconductor industry:

1. Solids that ignite upon contact with **water**
2. Solids that ignite or explode upon contact with **air**
3. Solids that ignite by **spark or friction**

### Examples

- Sodium metal
- White phosphorus (from implanters)
- Yellow phosphorus (from implanters)
- Dichlorosilane and trichlorosilane byproducts (epitaxy and polysilicon depositions)

### Controls

- Used in closed systems
- Rigorous storage, handling, and disposal measures
- Fires require specialized **Class D (metals) fire extinguishers**
- Only trained personnel should attempt to extinguish flammable solid fires

---

## 5. Combustible Chemicals

Unlike flammable liquids, combustible chemicals **do not release flammable vapors at room temperature**. However, they will release ignitable vapors if heated.

### Examples

- **Liquids:** Oils, diesel
- **Solids:** Furniture, plastics, cardboard

### Storage Rules

- Store combustible liquids in sealed containers and flammable storage cabinets
- Keep combustible solids away from heat sources and open flames
- Flammable and combustible liquids may be stored together
- Do NOT store flammable solids with flammable/combustible liquids (solids can become an ignition source)

---

## 6. Pyrophoric Chemicals

A pyrophoric chemical **spontaneously ignites in air** at or below 130°F (54°C).

### Examples

- **Gas:** Silane
- **Liquids:** Organometallic liquids (trimethyl aluminum, DMAH)
- **Solids:** Yellow phosphorus, residues in exhaust lines and ducts

### Controls

- Closed systems for all pyrophoric solids, liquids, and gases
- Strict storage and use procedures

### Emergency Response

- Handle emergencies through **remote means** (emergency off button, closing chemical in hood)
- **Do NOT use an extinguisher** — some extinguishing materials can react with pyrophoric materials
- Move away and call for trained emergency responders

---

## 7. Fire Emergency Actions Summary

| Hazard | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Flammable liquid fire | Leave the area if possible. If liquid on skin/clothing, remove clothing and rinse. |
| Flammable gas release / alarm | **Leave the area immediately** |
| Flammable solid fire | Only trained personnel should extinguish (Class D extinguisher). Leave if untrained. |
| Pyrophoric chemical fire | Do NOT use extinguisher. Use remote shutoff. Move away, call emergency responders. |

---

## 8. Oxidizers

Oxidizers **do not burn**, but they make fuels burn better. Some oxidizers are so strong that an ignition source is not necessary to create fire.

### Examples

- Nitric acid
- Hydrogen peroxide
- Chlorine
- 100% oxygen
- Nitrous oxide, nitric oxide
- Fluorine

### Controls

- Keep oxidizers and flammables **separated**
- Keep ignition sources away from oxidizers and flammables

### Emergency: Skin Contact with Oxidizer or Corrosive

- **Rinse the area with water immediately**
- Know the location of safety showers
- Decontaminate a person **before** transporting to another location or ambulance

---

## 9. Corrosive Chemicals (Acids and Bases)

Used in etching, cleaning, photolithography, and equipment/facility maintenance.

### Specific Chemical Hazards

| Chemical | Effects |
|----------|---------|
| **Hydrofluoric acid (HF)** | Extremely corrosive. Deep penetrating burns. Respiratory irritation, eye damage, pulmonary edema, bone damage. |
| **Sulfuric acid** | Burns any contact area. Can cause blindness. Irreversible damage if ingested or inhaled. |
| **Nitric acid** | Extremely painful burn on contact. May be lethal if inhaled. |
| **Sodium hydroxide** | Strong base. At high concentrations burns tissue. May NOT feel painful on contact (feels slippery — dissolving fat under skin). |

### Controls

- Control vapors
- Use corrosion-resistant materials and containers
- Avoid mixing incompatible materials
- Wear corrosion-resistant PPE

### HF Exposure First Aid (Special Steps)

1. **Call emergency services** immediately — transfer to ER as soon as possible
2. **Rinse** contaminated area under safety shower for **at least 5 minutes**
3. **Remove all contaminated clothing** while in the shower (clothing absorbs the acid)
4. Using a gloved hand, **apply calcium gluconate gel** to the burn

## 10. Toxic Chemicals

### Examples in Semiconductor Fabrication

- Ammonia
- Arsenic, Arsine
- Diborane
- Germane
- Phosphine
- Ozone

### Controls

- Ventilated chemical hoods
- Closed systems
- Supplied air respirators
- Gloves and protective clothing

### Exposure Factors

Toxicity depends on:
- **Dose** (amount of exposure)
- **Duration** (how long)
- **Route of entry** (inhalation, absorption, ingestion, injection)
- Overall health and immune system

### Exposure Limits

| Acronym | Term | Description |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **PEL** | Permissible Exposure Limit | Set by the government |
| **TLV** | Threshold Limit Value | Updated yearly by independent lab. Use when available. |
| **TWA** | Time-Weighted Average | Acceptable exposure for an 8-hour workday |
| **Ceiling** | Ceiling Limit | Cannot be exceeded for any period of time |
| **STEL** | Short-Term Exposure Level | Allowable exposure for 15 minutes |
| **IDLH** | Immediately Dangerous to Life and Health | Exposure causes irreversible health effects, possibly death |

> PEL, TLV, TWA, STEL = allowable levels (relatively safe).
> IDLH = level at which you are **NOT safe**.

### Toxic Chemical Emergency Response

| Route of Exposure | Action |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Inhalation** | Move patient to fresh air, call emergency services |
| **Skin absorption** | Rinse affected area with water, call emergency services |
| **Ingestion** | Call poison control center, then call emergency services |
| **Injection** | Control bleeding, call emergency services |

## 11. Chemical Storage and Compatibility

### Storage Rules by Type

| Chemical Type | Storage |
|---------------|---------|
| Flammable liquids | Flammable chemical cabinet |
| Flammable gases | Chemical cabinet or well-ventilated area |
| Flammable solids | Chemical cabinet (NOT with flammable liquids) |
| Pyrophoric gases | Approved gas cabinet |
| Pyrophoric liquids/solids | Same as flammable solids |
| Combustible liquids | Flammable chemical cabinet |
| Oxidizers | Chemical cabinet, separated from flammable/combustible materials |
| Corrosive liquids/solids | Corrosive chemical cabinet (do NOT store acids and bases on same shelf) |
| Corrosive gases | Approved gas cabinet |
| Toxic liquids | Chemical cabinet |
| Toxic gases | Approved gas cabinet |

### Compatibility Chart

| | Flammable | Oxidizer | Acid | Base | Toxic |
|---|:---------:|:--------:|:----:|:----:|:-----:|
| **Flammable** | OK | NO | NO | NO | OK |
| **Oxidizer** | NO | OK | OK | NO | NO |
| **Acid** | NO | OK | OK | NO | NO |
| **Base** | NO | NO | NO | OK | NO |
| **Toxic** | OK | NO | NO | NO | OK |

## 12. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

- **Safety glasses, goggles, face shields** — appropriately rated
- **Hand protection** — check manufacturer's glove chart for chemical resistance rating
- **Chemical respirators** — may require specialized program, medical exams, equipment fitting, and training
- **Aprons, sleeves, chemical protective clothing** — check manufacturer's ratings for the specific chemical
- **Closed-toe shoes** when handling chemicals
